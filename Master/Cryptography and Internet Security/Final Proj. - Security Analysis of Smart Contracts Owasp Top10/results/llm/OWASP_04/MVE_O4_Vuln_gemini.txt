### 1. Vulnerability Type: Economic Manipulation / Flash Loan Attack / Bonus Abuse

*   **Severity:** Critical
*   **Affected Function:** `deposit()` (lines 18-20), enabled by `withdrawAll()`
*   **Brief Description of the Risk:** The contract offers an immediate 1 Ether bonus for any deposit of 10 Ether or more. An attacker can leverage this by using temporary capital (e.g., a flash loan) to deposit 10 Ether, immediately claim the 1 Ether bonus, and then withdraw their entire principal (10 Ether) plus the bonus (1 Ether) in quick succession or within the same transaction. They can then repay the flash loan (10 Ether + fee) and keep the 1 Ether bonus (minus fee). This process can be repeated, allowing an attacker to drain the contract's funds by continuously claiming bonuses without holding the required principal for any significant duration. The contract's entire balance, including funds from `fundPool` or other users' `principal`, is at risk of being depleted.
*   **Recommended Fix:** Implement a time lock or vesting period for the bonus, requiring the principal to remain staked for a certain duration (e.g., X blocks/days) before the bonus becomes withdrawable. Alternatively, the bonus mechanism could be redesigned to be pro-rata based on time held or removed entirely if instant withdrawal is intended.

### 2. Vulnerability Type: Denial of Service (DoS) for Specific Users

*   **Severity:** Medium
*   **Affected Function:** `withdrawAll()` (line 30)
*   **Brief Description of the Risk:** The `withdrawAll` function uses a low-level `call` to send Ether to `msg.sender` and `require`s the call to succeed. If `msg.sender` is a smart contract that either does not have a `receive()` or `fallback()` function capable of receiving Ether, or if its `receive()`/`fallback()` function explicitly reverts, the `call` will fail. The subsequent `require(ok, "transfer failed");` will then revert the entire `withdrawAll` transaction. While this prevents the user from losing their funds (as state changes are reverted), it effectively denies that specific user the ability to withdraw any funds from the contract, making their assets inaccessible.
*   **Recommended Fix:** Consider implementing a pull-based withdrawal system where users' contracts can call a `claim()` function on this contract to pull funds, or allow users to set a designated safe withdrawal address. For a simpler fix, add a `try-catch` block around the `call` to handle failures gracefully (e.g., log the failure and allow users to try again or claim to a different address) without reverting the entire transaction for all users.

### One-sentence summary of the most critical finding:
The immediate and instantly withdrawable bonus mechanism in the `deposit` function creates a severe economic vulnerability, enabling attackers to drain the contract's entire fund pool through repeated flash loan-powered bonus claims.
